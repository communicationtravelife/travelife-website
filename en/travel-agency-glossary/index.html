Key facts The travel agency glossary in six points

  • DistributionAgencies access flights through GDSs (Amadeus, Sabre, Travelport) and, increasingly, through IATA's NDC standard.
  • BookingAn air booking is stored in a PNR, identified by a booking reference, and then leads to the issue of an e-ticket.
  • AccommodationRooms are bought directly, as an allotment with a release date, or from wholesale accommodation providers known as bedbanks.
  • DocumentsA booking file moves through quote, proforma, invoice, voucher and, for groups, rooming list.
  • FinanceAirline tickets are settled through IATA's BSP, and package holidays often fall under the VAT on margin scheme, depending on each country's regulations.
  • SoftwareAn agency ERP connects front office, mid office and back office so that a booking becomes an invoiced booking file without re-keying.

Digital

AI Agent

An AI agent is a software assistant powered by artificial intelligence that understands customer requests in natural language, answers them and can take action in the agency's tools: qualifying a lead, preparing a proposal, sending a follow-up. Unlike a chatbot with fixed scripts, it adapts to the context of the conversation.

Allotment

An allotment is a block of rooms, seats or places reserved in advance by an agency or tour operator from a supplier, at negotiated terms. Unsold units must be released back before the release date.

Distribution

Amadeus

Amadeus is one of the leading GDSs used by travel agencies to search, book and issue airline tickets, as well as other services such as hotels and car rentals.

See also: GDS
Digital

API

An API (application programming interface) lets two software systems exchange data automatically. In tourism, APIs connect the agency to airlines, hotel wholesalers and inbound agencies to display availability and book in real time.

Products

Travel insurance

Travel insurance covers travelers against certain risks related to their trip: cancellation, medical expenses, repatriation, baggage. It can be sold on its own or added to a package, and is often among the supporting documents required for a visa.

Finance

Credit note

A credit note is an accounting document that cancels all or part of an invoice already issued, for example after a cancellation or a refund. It is linked to the original invoice and reduces the amount owed by the customer.

Distribution

B2B

B2B (business to business) refers to sales between professionals. For an agency or tour operator, it means distributing its products to a network of reseller agencies, usually through a secure booking portal.

The B2B booking system
Distribution

B2C

B2C (business to consumer) refers to direct sales to individual customers. An agency sells B2C over the counter, by phone, through messaging apps or on its own online booking website.

Back office

The back office covers internal functions the customer never sees: invoicing, payments, supplier purchases, accounting, VAT and reporting. In a travel agency, it is usually handled by the ERP.

Finance

BSP

The BSP (Billing and Settlement Plan) is IATA's system that centralizes the billing and settlement of airline tickets between accredited agencies and airlines. The agency periodically pays one consolidated amount instead of paying each airline separately.

7 terms
Distribution

Bedbank (hotel wholesaler)

A hotel wholesaler, or bedbank, aggregates rooms from many hotels and resells them to agencies, usually via API. It provides access to a large inventory without signing a contract with each property.

Software

Channel manager

A channel manager is software that synchronizes a property's availability and rates across several sales channels: OTAs, direct website, agencies. It reduces overbooking by updating every channel with each booking.

Digital

Chatbot

A chatbot is a program that automatically converses with visitors on a website or messaging app. Traditional chatbots follow predefined scripts; the latest ones rely on artificial intelligence, like an AI agent.

Products

Tour (circuit)

A tour is a multi-stop trip that follows a set itinerary, often with a guide. It usually combines transport, accommodation, sightseeing and meals in a single booking file.

Cloud

The cloud refers to hosting software and its data on remote servers accessed over the internet. The agency uses the application from a web browser, with nothing to install and no server to manage on site.

Commission

A commission is the fee paid to an intermediary, often calculated as a percentage of the selling price. An agency may earn commissions from its suppliers or pay them to its B2B resellers.

CRM

A CRM (Customer Relationship Management) system centralizes contacts, requests, communication history and sales follow-up. It helps the agency turn inquiries into sales and build customer loyalty.

Quote

A quote is the priced sales proposal given to the customer before the sale, detailing services, prices and terms. Once accepted, it is converted into an order and then an invoice.

Distribution

DMC

A DMC (Destination Management Company) is a local inbound agency that designs and operates services at its destination: transfers, excursions, accommodation, guides, events. It mainly works for foreign agencies and tour operators.

E-ticket (electronic ticket)

An e-ticket is the paperless version of an airline ticket, stored in the airline's systems and identified by a ticket number. The traveler receives an itinerary receipt summarizing the trip.

Documents

EMD

An EMD (Electronic Miscellaneous Document) is an electronic document issued for ancillary services related to an airline ticket: extra baggage, seat selection, change fees, upgrades. It is linked to the passenger's ticket or booking.

Software

ERP

An ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning) is an integrated management software suite that brings bookings, sales, purchasing, invoicing and accounting together in one tool. For a travel agency, it eliminates double data entry between departments.

NDC

NDC (New Distribution Capability) is an IATA standard that lets airlines distribute their offers to agencies with richer content: personalized fares, options, ancillary services. It complements traditional GDS distribution.

No-show

A no-show is a customer who does not turn up for a booked service (flight, hotel, excursion) without having canceled. Depending on the fare conditions, it can result in fees or loss of the service.

Umrah

Umrah is the lesser pilgrimage to Mecca, which can be performed at any time of year outside the Hajj period. An Umrah program combines visa, flights, hotels in Mecca and Medina, transfers and group supervision.

Distribution

OTA

An OTA (Online Travel Agency) is a travel agency that sells exclusively online, through its website or app. It relies on booking engines connected to large inventories of flights and accommodations.

Booking

Overbooking

Overbooking means selling more seats or rooms than the available capacity, anticipating cancellations and no-shows. When all customers turn up, the supplier has to rebook or compensate some of them.

PNR

The PNR (Passenger Name Record) is the booking record created in a GDS or an airline's system. It contains passenger names, itinerary, contacts and fare details, and is identified by a booking reference code.

Documents

Proforma

A proforma invoice is a provisional document that presents the details and amount of an order before the final invoice. It is often used to request a deposit or to build an administrative file.

What is a GDS in a travel agency?

A GDS (Global Distribution System) is a global booking system that gives agencies real-time access to flights from many airlines, as well as hotels and car rentals. Amadeus, Sabre and Travelport are the main GDS.

What is the difference between GDS and NDC?

A GDS is a technical intermediary that aggregates the offers of many airlines. NDC is an IATA standard that lets airlines send their offers to agencies with richer content (personalized fares, options, ancillary services), with or without going through a GDS. Both channels coexist today.

What is the difference between a travel agency, a tour operator and a DMC?

A travel agency sells trips and services to its customers. A tour operator designs packages by assembling transport, accommodation and services, and sells them directly or through agencies. A DMC is a local inbound operator that delivers the services on the ground on behalf of agencies and tour operators.

What do front-office, mid-office and back-office mean?

The front-office covers the tools in contact with the customer (counter, B2C website, B2B portal). The mid-office turns the booking into a complete file (PNR, vouchers, documents). The back-office handles invoicing, payments, accounting and VAT. A travel agency ERP links these three levels.

What is the difference between allotment and release?

An allotment is a stock of rooms or seats reserved in advance with a supplier. The release is the deadline by which the agency must return the units it has not sold; after this deadline, they may be charged to the agency according to the contract.

What is the difference between a voucher and an invoice?

The invoice is the accounting document that sets the amount owed by the customer. The voucher is the exchange coupon that proves a service has been booked and paid for: the traveler presents it to the hotel or provider. In Travelife Manage, both are generated from the same booking file.

What is VAT on margin for a travel agency?

It is a special scheme, applicable in many countries, under which an agency that resells in its own name services bought from third parties is taxed on its margin and not on the total price. The rate and rules depend on the regulations in force. See our page on VAT on margin software.
